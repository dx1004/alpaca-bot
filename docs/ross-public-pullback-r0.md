## $500 arithmetic, not an allocated risk budget

For supplied entry `P > 0`, stop `0 < S < P`, and usable amount `B`, a whole-share affordability ceiling is `floor(B / P)`. A proposed position would additionally have to satisfy a **separately established** risk budget `R`: `q <= min(floor(B / P), floor(R / (P - S)))`. This inequality is derived arithmetic, not Ross's complete position-sizing algorithm. It neither selects `R` nor says to use the entire ceiling. Costs, price movement, holds and asset restrictions can lower usable buying power; a stop price does not cap realized loss when fills slip.

With **hypothetical B = $500 before costs/holds**, Decimal arithmetic checked:

| Input example | Affordable ceiling | Notional / remainder | Nominal loss at stated stop |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| STOP: P=$4.50, S=$4.33 | 111 shares | $499.50 / $0.50 | $18.87 |
| AHPI approximation: P=$10.75, S=$10.21 | 46 shares | $494.50 / $5.50 | $24.84 |

For comparison only, 5%/10% of $500 is $25/$50; of $1,000 is $50/$100; of $2,000 is $100/$200. Numerical coincidence at $1,000 does not prove TK25's fixed-dollar table was intended for that account or stage. No risk/share/target value was added to runtime configuration.
